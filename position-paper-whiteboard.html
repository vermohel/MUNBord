1. What is a position paper?2 minutes

Position paper = one page, your country's answer to one question:

What should this committee do about this topic, and why?

Who reads it

  1. Chairs. They grade it and use it to spot who prepared.
  2. Other delegates. They read it to find allies.

Ask: who reads it, and why? Move on when someone gives both.

2. Your assignment2 minutes. Everything you write today is for this card.

Committee

OPEC (Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries)

Topic

Oil supply crisis after the closure of the Strait of Hormuz

Questions the committee must answer

  1. How should OPEC decide which members make up the barrels lost while the Strait is closed?
  2. Should members whose shortfall is not their fault be treated differently from members who chose to cut?
  3. Who pays for emergency measures, and by what formula?

Your country: Saudi Arabia

Facts with numbers

  • About 267 billion barrels of proven reserves, second in OPEC after Venezuela.
  • Produces around 9 million barrels a day; can produce about 12 million. Roughly 3 million a day of spare capacity, the most of any member.
  • Owns the East-West Pipeline to Yanbu on the Red Sea, about 7 million barrels a day, which bypasses Hormuz.

What it has done

  • After the September 2019 Abqaiq attack cut output by about 5.7 million barrels a day, it restored full production within weeks.
  • Led OPEC+ voluntary production cuts since 2022.
  • Expanded the East-West Pipeline for exactly this kind of emergency.

Won't accept

Any rule that takes production share from members who can produce and hands it to members who cannot, or that lets OPEC order Saudi spare capacity into use.

4. Voice1 minute

Never I, we, our.

The speaker is the delegation of Saudi Arabia.

The words Saudi Arabia appear in every paragraph.

Verbs that work: proposes, urges, believes, recognizes, will not support.

Rewrite this

We believe our pipeline should be used by everyone.

I think the crisis is bad for everyone.

Our reserves are the second largest.

We won't accept losing our share.

5. How to research2 minutes

Read in this order

  1. The study guide. Go to the questions first.
  2. Your country's numbers on this topic.
  3. What your country did or said.
  4. What the committee already decided.

Write down

One line per fact: fact + number or date + source.

Stop when

One sentence per committee question, 3 numbers, 2 past actions. About 2 hours.

Example note

6. Solutions: four questions5 minutes. A wish becomes a solution when all four are answered.

QuestionA. Venezuela (model)B. Saudi Arabia (yours)
What exactly? A Supply Shock Compensation Fund
Who runs it? (existing body) OPEC's existing Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee
Who pays, by what formula? When prices spike, every member pays a small levy on the windfall (the extra revenue from the price jump)
Who checks, how often, with what consequence? The Monitoring Committee, monthly, using production data OPEC already collects. Proven involuntary shortfalls get paid; others do not.

7. Mistakes that cost the most2 minutes

  1. Generic background, country appears late. Fix: country in sentence one.
  2. I, we, our. Fix: the delegation of.
  3. No numbers. Fix: three minimum.
  4. Solution with no runner, no formula, no check. Fix: the four questions.

Which mistake is this?

We think OPEC should work together to solve the crisis.

  1. Saying what the world should do, not what your country wants.
  2. Inventing a new body when one exists.
  3. Copying study guide sentences.
  4. Ignoring the committee's questions.
  5. Contradicting your country's real behavior.
  6. Vague verbs: address, tackle, raise awareness.

8. Write one solution paragraph4 minutes solo, then swap and check for 2

Your paragraph

As the delegation of Saudi Arabia. Use column B from zone 6. Four to six sentences. First words: The delegation of Saudi Arabia proposes

Pair check: tick each

  1. Starts with "The delegation of Saudi Arabia"
  2. No I, we, our
  3. Names the thing
  4. Names an existing body that runs it
  5. Says who pays and by what formula
  6. Says who checks, how often, and what happens to a cheater

The delegation of Saudi Arabia proposes an Emergency Red Sea Routing Agreement under which member states export through the Saudi East-West Pipeline to Yanbu while the Strait of Hormuz is closed. The existing OPEC Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee would allocate pipeline slots each month in proportion to each member's lost export volume. Members using the route would pay a per-barrel transit fee, set by the Secretariat at the pipeline's operating cost plus 5 percent, so that Saudi Arabia does not carry the cost alone. The Monitoring Committee would review routed volumes every month against OPEC's own secondary-source production data, and any member found routing barrels above its declared shortfall would lose its slot for the following month.

9. Final check3 minutes writing, alone

Saudi Arabia, question 3: who pays for emergency measures, and by what formula?

Four short lines:

  1. What
  2. Who runs it

  4. Who checks, and the consequence

Scoring for you: 4 lines complete = got it. 2 or 3 = nearly. 0 or 1 = redo with you this week. Beginners drop formula and consequence.
